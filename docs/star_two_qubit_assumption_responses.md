# Responses to the two-qubit STAR modeling assumptions

This record states the adopted treatment of each uncertainty in the optimistic
STAR resource comparison and its implementation status. The purpose is to test
whether STAR's early fault-tolerant utility remains limited when uncertain
logical-error penalties are omitted in STAR's favor. These idealizations do not
make the entire estimator a proven upper bound on physical capability.

## Current status

Assumptions **1–5 and 7 are implemented in the main two-qubit estimator**.
Assumption **6 is partial**: shared surface-code conventions are implemented,
while STAR-specific growth, storage, and adaptive scheduling remain provisional.
The direct and surface-code implementations and their configuration are unchanged.

| Assumption | Adopted treatment | Implementation status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Additional preparation logical errors | Set the additional preparation allowance to zero | Implemented in `STAR_BASELINE` |
| 2. Rejection versus accepted quality | Counted $A$ and $b_1=a_eT_1/A$; idealize other accepted logical effects | Implemented in `counted_quantities` |
| 3. Rejection probabilities | All-orders acceptance for the specified circuits; charge rejected attempts | Implemented using packaged circuit inputs and geometric retry costs |
| 4. Physical fault formula | Retain $k(2p/15+q/3)$ and background survival in $T_1$ | Implemented and checked against source weights |
| 5. Original STAR and coherent corrections | Retain counted original-STAR logical error; grant ideal Toshio auxiliary rotations with supply costs | Implemented, including distance-dependent search |
| 6. Architecture and scheduling | Reuse shared conventions; retain provisional STAR-specific factory and scheduling costs | Partial |
| 7. Deadline bound | Compare mean runtime with one week, without Markov scaling or deadline-error subtraction | Implemented directly |

## Remaining work under the shared-baseline criterion

The surface-code baseline treats factories as sources described by output error,
footprint, and effective output period. It does not provide a detailed calibration
of STAR preparation. Shared routing, patch-memory law, injection/frame conventions,
and estimated-runtime accounting therefore use the same assumptions in both models.
Protocol-specific errors and yields come from the specified STAR circuits.

Remaining work is to connect those circuits to effective factory time/footprint
and feasible adaptive supply overlap (assumption 6). The current distance search,
odd $d=3..31$, also differs from the surface-code range; align it or establish
that it is nonbinding before claiming an optimum over that larger menu. Counted
inputs reject unsupported rates, distances, and preparation settings rather than
extrapolating them. The native-RZZ sensitivity and physical calibration of omitted
logical channels are outside the adopted comparison.

## 1. Additional logical errors during accepted preparation

Set $\gamma=0$ and $\ell_p=0$, implemented by
`STAR_BASELINE.preparation_floor_scale=0`. This applies to every accepted state.
It removes an uncalibrated extra allowance for background logical failures from
idles or faulty syndrome extraction; it does not establish that these failures
physically vanish. Explicit main-state errors and live-patch memory exposure remain.
The surface-code baseline similarly uses factory-output errors without adding
this generic preparation allowance.

> To obtain an optimistic estimate of STAR’s capability, we neglect additional
> logical errors from idle noise or faulty syndrome extraction during accepted
> resource-state preparation, while retaining the explicitly modeled rotation-angle
> errors and the identified logical errors in original-STAR states used for protocol
> switching; auxiliary rotations for probabilistic coherent cancellation are
> assumed exact, with their preparation, retry, and runtime costs retained.

`STAR_LEGACY` and the historical single-qubit configuration retain their old
allowance. The main two-qubit default and all adopted saved rows use zero.

## 2. Separating rejection from conditional state quality

For the specified compiled Toshio circuit, use

$$
A=\sum_n a_nH_n,\qquad b_1=\frac{a_eT_1}{A}.
$$

Acceptance includes every counted fault order. $T_1$ contains exactly one
nonzero signature in the known one-group sector, marginalizing other
zero-signature faults. Retain its established angle offset $\Delta$ and assign
other accepted histories ideal logical output. This replaces the historical
common-$h$ cancellation and the proposal to give every accepted pair the same
single-fault output angle. No ideal-syndrome-extraction assumption is made.

The corrected main error is $2b_1(1-b_1)\sin^2\Delta$ in the preparation angle
convention. The sensitivity adds

$$
v=\frac{\sum_{n\ge1}a_nH_n-a_eT_1}{A}
$$

before recomputing switching and resource allocation. It charges omitted nonzero
Toshio sectors as failures without assigning their output angles. It preserves
the plateaus above but increases qubit costs. It leaves zero-sector and unmodeled
original-STAR effects idealized, so it is not a full-channel bound or a uniform
accuracy guarantee at smaller budgets.

## 3. Circuit-derived rejection probabilities

Use the counted all-orders acceptance of both protocols. Toshio sector weights
come from deterministic Fourier transfer; original-STAR acceptance comes from
an exhaustive Fourier sum at distance 3 and positive-integrand sampling at larger
distances, with recorded numerical standard errors. The original-STAR product
extrapolation of first-order rejection counts is no longer the main input.

Rejected attempts consume modeled preparation time and expose live patches to
memory errors. Free replacement states and unit acceptance are not adopted.
The [Toshio](star_rejection_count_derivation.md) and
[original-STAR](original_star_rejection_count_derivation.md) derivations specify
the circuits. A changed schedule, native gate, or physical-rate model needs new
acceptance data.

## 4. Physical fault formula and higher orders

Retain $u=k(2p/15+q/3)$. A Z fault after the physical RZ gate has probability
$q/3$ and propagates through the second CNOT to ZZ. The $kq/3$ contribution enters
the retained weight $T_1$ and hence $b_1$; it neither changes $\Delta$ nor adds a
separate direct circuit-failure charge. The exact background product is retained
in the numerator, and all counted sectors enter acceptance.

Other accepted logical effects are idealized. Neglecting terms in a general
coherent/postselected expansion is not automatically a rigorous favorable bound;
the adopted model states which histories receive ideal output instead.

## 5. Original STAR for protocol switching and coherent corrections

The [accepted-state derivation](original_star_channel_and_correction.md) identifies
four CNOT Pauli faults and one physical-RZ fault giving logical Z in the specified
seed/expansion circuit. Use $p_f=W_{1Z}/A_{\rm orig}$, with leading coefficient
$4p/15+q/3$, while idealizing other accepted logical effects. This includes the
unresolved decoder outcome of residual output syndromes. The leading value is
$6\times10^{-4}$ on H1 and $2.67\times10^{-4}$ on H3, before finite-noise conditioning.
It is a circuit-specific retained component, not a complete physical error rate.

For auxiliary coherent cancellation, follow Toshio's transversal RUS construction
and grant ideal correction output. Remove $b_1\,2p_f/(1+p_f)$, retaining main
dephasing $2b_1(1-b_1)\sin^2\Delta$. Main switching still uses nonzero $p_f$.
Corrections retain acceptance at their actual angle and successive doubled RUS
angles, failed attempts, injection time, and live-patch memory. They use the same
$k$, without auxiliary original-STAR switching or recursive cancellation.

This idealization deliberately favors STAR and goes beyond merely granting free
software Clifford frames. Ideal corrections alone leave the previous plateaus
unchanged; the revised original-STAR inputs raise H3 from 2,744 to 3,136.

## 6. Growth, delivery, layout, and scheduling

The implemented shared conventions are $2w$ data/routing patches, $2d^2$ physical
qubits per patch, the common patch-error fit and cycle time, parallel CNOT layers
in $2d$ cycles, free delivery, one $d$-cycle injection, and software frame tracking.
All live data, routing, and required storage patches incur memory exposure.

Still provisional are $4+d$ cycles per preparation attempt, growth charges on
rejected attempts, the additional handoff/storage patch per station, synchronized
main batches, and serialized auxiliary rotations. The surface-code baseline
allows production/consumption overlap, but its T-state supply formula does not
resolve adaptive STAR angle dependencies. A feasible replacement for these
STAR-specific allowances and scheduling restrictions remains to be developed.
They are not marked solved by the shared routing assumption.

## 7. A deliberately loose deadline bound

The main `deadline_model='mean'` directly applies
$K\lfloor W/\mathbb E[T]\rfloor\ge M$ for $M$ executions and $K$ copies.
It retains preparation retries, RUS, and their memory exposure. It removes the
STAR-only $10^6$ Markov runtime factor and the extra deadline-failure subtraction
from success. The unused `deadline_failure` parameter has no effect in this mode.

This matches the baseline's estimated-runtime convention, not a deadline-tail
guarantee. Explicit legacy scenarios can still select `deadline_model='markov'`.
